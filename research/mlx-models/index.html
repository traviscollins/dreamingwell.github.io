The strongest thing that actually runs. glm4_moe has zero open issues in mlx-lm - every one closed - and the 4-bit MLX quant already exists. It uses conventional attention rather than hybrid linear-attention, so it should sidestep the prefix-cache bug that cripples Qwen3.8-27B; confirm that first, it is the main reason to prefer it. Reading the scores

  • Terminal-Bench 2.0 and 2.1 are different benchmarks. GLM-4.7's 41.0 is on v2.0; Qwen3.8-27B's 73.0 and GLM-5.2's 81.0 are on v2.1. Do not rank across the two — they are shown labelled, not normalised.
  • Scores are vendor-reported or aggregator-reported, not reproduced here. Treat them as a shortlist filter, then verify the shortlist on your own context-rot harness.
  • Nothing on this page has been measured on M5 Ultra hardware. Footprints are arithmetic; everything else is published numbers.

Where this leaves the plan

  • Lead with GLM-4.7. It is the only model that is simultaneously strong, fits one box, MIT-licensed, and sitting on a mature MLX path with zero open architecture issues. It also likely avoids the prefix-cache bug entirely, because it does not use hybrid linear attention.
  • Pair it with GLM-4.7-Flash as a cheap tier — 3B active, same code path, same quant family — and route by task stakes rather than running one model for everything.
  • Keep Qwen3.8-27B as the control arm, not the plan. vllm-mlx #731 merging would restore its prefix caching; until then its published Terminal-Bench 2.1 of 73.0 is the best agentic number among things that run.
  • DeepSeek V4 Flash remains the best theoretical fit but needs a new architecture upstream, not a bug fix. GLM-5.2 is the quality ceiling and is blocked three ways.
  • Sizing figures remain unverified. Every M5 Ultra number is a bandwidth and compute model, not a measurement. Re-derive on day one, multiple runs per arm, and start with a memory-stability soak rather than a throughput benchmark.
